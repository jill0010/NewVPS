Deployment

Run a Litecoin full node in 5 steps

From order to synced node. Commands are copy-paste ready for Ubuntu 24.04 — adapt paths if you run Debian or Rocky.

  1. 1

    Order the server

    VPS-XL recommended; pick Moldova for the lowest price or the Netherlands for peering.

  2. 2

    Basic hardening

    Key-only SSH, firewall open on 9333, automatic security updates. Ten minutes, done once.

  3. 3

    Install Litecoin Core

    Download from litecoin.org, verify the signatures, install the binaries.

    wget https://download.litecoin.org/litecoin-0.21.4/linux/litecoin-0.21.4-x86_64-linux-gnu.tar.gz
tar xzf litecoin-0.21.4-x86_64-linux-gnu.tar.gz
install -m 0755 litecoin-0.21.4/bin/* /usr/local/bin/
  4. 4

    Configure & sync

    server=1, dbcache sized to your RAM, daemon mode. Start litecoind and let it chew through the chain.

    # ~/.litecoin/litecoin.conf
server=1
dbcache=4000
daemon=1
  5. 5

    Verify & connect wallets

    getblockchaininfo confirms the tip; then point Litecoin Core GUI, Electrum-LTC or your payment stack at your own RPC.

    litecoind
litecoin-cli getblockchaininfo

Litecoin node requirements

The chain sits around 230 GB in mid-2026 and grows a gentle ~25 GB a year — the friendliest curve of any major PoW coin. 4 GB RAM runs it; 8 GB makes it comfortable; anything more becomes dbcache. Any of our NVMe tiers outruns the sync workload, so the choice is really disk headroom: 200 GB pruned on VPS-L, 400 GB full on VPS-XL, or bare metal if Litecoin shares the box with its big brother.

MWEB: validate Litecoin's privacy layer yourself

Since 2022, MWEB lets LTC move with confidential amounts inside extension blocks. Wallets that support it rely on a node that validates the MWEB data — running your own means those confidential balances rest on your CPU's word, not an API's. It's enabled by default in Litecoin Core: your node checks both chains, transparent and confidential, from block one.

The merchant angle: LTC without middlemen

Low fees and 2.5-minute blocks keep Litecoin popular at crypto-only checkouts. Wire your store's payment detection to your own node's RPC — getreceivedbyaddress, ZMQ notifications — and payment confirmation becomes a private, first-party fact: no explorer rate limits during a sale rush, no third party learning your revenue curve. Our own checkout accepts LTC for exactly these reasons.

One box, several nodes

Litecoin's lightness makes it the classic second tenant: DS-1 runs Bitcoin + Litecoin full nodes together with room left for electrs on both. If you're building multi-coin infrastructure — payment processing, an explorer, arbitrage tooling — start from the node hosting hub and stack chains by disk budget. LTC is the one you'll never have to think about again.

FAQ

Litecoin node hosting FAQ

01 What does a Litecoin node cost to host?

Pruned from $17/mo (VPS-L), full chain from $24.50/mo (VPS-XL) — cheapest in Moldova. That includes unmetered relaying; there is no bandwidth math to do.

02 How long is the initial sync?

6–18 hours on our NVMe tiers depending on peers and dbcache. Start it in the evening; it's at the tip by morning.

03 Does my node validate MWEB transactions?

Yes — Litecoin Core validates extension blocks as part of consensus. Your node fully checks confidential transactions without any extra configuration.

04 Can I run Litecoin and Bitcoin on the same server?

That's the DS-1 use case: both chains fit on 2× 1 TB NVMe with years of headroom, sharing 64 GB ECC RAM comfortably. Different ports (8333/9333), zero conflicts.

05 Is a Litecoin node useful for mining?

Solo miners and pool operators need one — and merged-mined Dogecoin rides on Litecoin infrastructure via auxpow. Hobby CPU mining isn't profitable, but your node serves external ASICs fine.

06 Why not just use an explorer API?

Explorers log queries, rate-limit, and occasionally mislead (reorgs, delayed mempools). A $17 node answers instantly, privately, from consensus-validated data. For anything money-touching, that's the whole point.
