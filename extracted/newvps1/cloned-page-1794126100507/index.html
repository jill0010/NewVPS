Why crypto, not cards

Credit cards create a KYC chain we cannot break

Accepting cards means integrating a payment processor — Stripe, Adyen, PayPal. Each of them enforces KYC on the merchant and stores the cardholder's identity on their side. Even if we never saw your name, they would. Crypto-only payments are the only way to deliver an end-to-end no-KYC guarantee without an intermediary breaking it.

The problem with "pay with Bitcoin" at mainstream hosts

Many large providers added a "pay with Bitcoin" button, but behind it runs a third-party processor like BitPay or Coinbase Commerce. These services require the merchant to KYC, sometimes KYC the customer above certain thresholds, and always connect the payment to a tradeable identity on their side. You paid in crypto but the privacy outcome is identical to a bank transfer. Our integration is direct — the address you pay to is one of our wallets, with no processor in between.

Which crypto should you use?

Monero (XMR) gives you the strongest privacy — ring signatures and stealth addresses make on-chain analysis essentially impossible. Bitcoin is slower but universally supported and private on the protocol level if you avoid KYC sources. Ethereum is faster (seconds to minutes) but have higher address traceability. USDT and USDC are stable (you know exactly the dollar amount) but centralised. Litecoin trades off the same way as BTC at lower fees. TRON settles in seconds with low fees but ledger transparency is full. For maximum privacy choose XMR; for speed pick TRX; for price certainty pick USDT or USDC.

What our checkout does under the hood

When you choose a plan and a crypto, we generate a payment intent: USD amount converted at the current rate, plus a small random offset to make your transaction uniquely identifiable on-chain, rounded to protocol precision. You have 60 minutes to send exactly that amount to the displayed address. Our backend polls the relevant chain every few seconds and, once a matching incoming TX is seen, your server is provisioned automatically.

Privacy tips for your crypto payment

If you bought your crypto on a KYC exchange (Coinbase, Binance) and send directly to us, your purchase is pseudonymous but traceable. For stronger privacy, route through a non-custodial wallet for at least one hop, or purchase from a peer-to-peer source. On Bitcoin, using coin control and a fresh receiving address from your side helps. We never ask where your coins came from — but the blockchain remembers.

FAQ

Crypto payment FAQ

01 Which cryptocurrencies do you accept?

Bitcoin (BTC), Monero (XMR), Ethereum (ETH), Tether USDT (ERC-20, TRC-20), Solana (SOL), Litecoin (LTC), TRON (TRX). Lightning is on the roadmap but not yet supported.

02 Why is the amount odd (e.g. 0.00412 BTC)?

We add a tiny random offset at order time so that multiple simultaneous payments of the same nominal USD amount remain individually identifiable on-chain. The offset is cents-level and disclosed on your payment page.

03 What if the price moves during my 60-minute window?

The USD→crypto conversion is locked when the order is placed. You pay the exact amount shown, regardless of live price movement. Do not send a rounded or updated amount — send exactly what is displayed.

04 How long until my server is provisioned?

VPS: typically 2-5 minutes after the first blockchain confirmation. Dedicated: 15-60 minutes (physical hardware preparation). TRON and TRC-20 USDT confirmations are fastest (under 1 minute).

05 What if I send the wrong amount?

Underpayments: your order sits until you top it up or the 60-minute window expires, after which we credit the amount toward a new order via support. Overpayments: same process — we refund the difference to a wallet you specify.

06 Do you accept Lightning or Monero?

Monero (XMR) is fully supported as one of our 20 native coins — pick it from the dropdown at checkout. Lightning is not supported yet but is on our roadmap.
