FAQ

Dedicated Servers — Frequently Asked Questions

01 How is a dedicated server different from VPS for privacy?

A dedicated server is bare-metal hardware reserved entirely for you — no hypervisor, no neighbour tenants, no shared kernel. You can enable LUKS full-disk encryption at provisioning, replace the kernel, and disable diagnostic interfaces. For threat models that include side-channel or co-tenant attacks, dedicated removes a class of risk that a VPS cannot.

02 Can I bring my own ISO (Whonix, Qubes, pfSense, custom)?

Yes. Every dedicated plan supports custom ISO upload through the IPMI/KVM console. Whonix, Qubes OS, pfSense, OpenBSD, FreeBSD and any Linux distribution work. We do not validate ISO contents or fingerprint installs — your hardware, your OS choice.

03 How long does dedicated provisioning take?

Most stock plans (DS-1, DS-2, DS-3) are pre-racked and provision in 1–4 hours after payment confirmation. The flagship DS-4 (dual-socket EPYC 9354, 256 GB ECC) is built to order: 24–72 hours depending on jurisdiction. Custom hardware requests via /contact have a 5–10 business day lead time.

04 Is IPMI / KVM remote access really included on every plan?

Yes — out-of-band IPMI access ships with every dedicated plan. You get HTML5 KVM console, virtual media, power cycling and BIOS access, all served over a dedicated IPMI VLAN. This means full recovery without ever touching the OS, and full control over BIOS-level features such as Intel ME or AMD PSP disabling where the chipset allows.

05 What happens if a DMCA or copyright notice is filed?

It depends on the jurisdiction you chose. Iceland, Panama, Moldova, Romania and Russia routinely ignore DMCA notices because they are not US copyright law. Switzerland and the Netherlands handle takedowns case-by-case under their domestic process — never via DMCA. We never share customer identity with rightsholders because we never had it.

06 Can I order multiple dedicated servers under one account token?

Yes. The same access token can manage unlimited servers across all 7 jurisdictions. There is no fleet limit and no per-server agreement. Each server is billed independently — top up the prepaid balance with crypto and rotate hardware as your workload changes.
